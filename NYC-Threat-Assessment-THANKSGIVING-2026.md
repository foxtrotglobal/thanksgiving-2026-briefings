## 1. Bottom Line

**Overall rating: ELEVATED, unchanged from 30 October. For parade day itself, the most concrete hazard is the weather, not a known threat.** *(Moderate confidence.)*

No public FBI, DHS, or NYPD warning names a specific, credible threat to the parade or to NYC this week, and DHS still shows **no current NTAS advisory** [3]. The big U.S. strike campaign on Iran that was expected after the 3 Nov midterms has **not** happened; instead there is a tit-for-tat exchange in the Gulf, and President Trump now says he is inclined to resume strikes **after the new year** if talks fail [4][5][6]. A CNN report (16 Nov) that Iran is developing dirty-bomb or chemical attack options against officials in Washington is unverified; DHS and the FBI call the reporting largely "aspirational," while the Director of National Intelligence told Congress that Iranian threats have "dramatically increased" [1][2]. A nor'easter is forecast to bring rising winds during the parade, with balloons possibly flown low or grounded by late morning, and heavy rain, 40–50 mph gusts, and coastal flooding Thursday afternoon and evening [26][27][28]. Our team's practical risks are crowding, closures, transit disruption, storm travel, petty crime, and hoax or false-alarm panic, not a directed attack.

## 4. The 100th Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade, Thursday 26 November [FACT unless noted]

- **Timing:** Steps off **8:30 AM ET** and runs about three and a half hours, ending around noon. The first floats usually reach Midtown around 9:30 AM. Spectators line up from about 6 AM [14][15].
- **Route (2.5 miles):** Starts at W 77th St and Central Park West, south to Columbus Circle, east on Central Park South (W 59th St), south on **Sixth Avenue** to W 34th St, then west to Macy's Herald Square [15][16].
- **Scale:** The city expects **more than 3.5 million spectators** along the route [17]. Macy's says a record 100 inflatables; FOX 5 counts 38 balloons and 5 "balloonicles," 28 floats and 7 marching bands. NBC says more than 11,000 volunteers and participants [14][16].
- **Where the public cannot stand:** the start at W 77th; Columbus Circle; the east side of Central Park West; the north side of Central Park South; the TV broadcast zone on Sixth Ave from W 38th to W 34th St; and the Herald Square finish, including W 34th St between Broadway and Seventh Ave [19].
- **Balloon Inflation (Wed 25 Nov):** Public viewing 1–6 PM. Enter at W 72nd St and Columbus Ave through a security check; viewing between W 77th and W 81st Sts [19][20].
- **Prohibited items:** drones, backpacks, large bags, umbrellas, chairs, alcohol and e-cigarettes are listed for the inflation event. Macy's/FOX 5 list large bags, umbrellas, backpacks and strollers for the parade. Anyone entering viewing streets may be searched [18][19].
- **Midtown closures (DOT, subject to NYPD change):** Sixth Ave from 23rd to 42nd St (2 AM–3 PM) and from 42nd to 58th St (5 AM–3 PM). W 42nd and W 57th Sts closed from Fifth to Eighth Aves (5 AM–3 PM). W 59th St from Fifth Ave to Columbus Circle (1 AM–5 PM). Seventh Ave from 32nd to 42nd St (2 AM–**8 PM**). Broadway from 32nd to 42nd St (2 AM–5 PM). W 34th St from Fifth to Ninth Aves (9 PM Wed–3 PM Thu) [17].
- **Subway (Thu):** Sunday schedule with extra early service on the 1 and the 42 St Shuttle [19]. 81 St–Museum of Natural History and the 57 St station on Sixth Ave are closed 4–11 AM. Exits are restricted at 72 St and 59 St–Columbus Circle. Select entrances are closed on the Sixth Ave line and at Penn Station. LIRR and Metro-North add trains before and after the parade [20].
- **NYPD security posture:** A 2026-specific NYPD security briefing had not been published as of 23 Nov; the city usually holds one the day before. In 2025 the NYPD described thousands of officers on the route; ESU, Mounted, K-9, Harbor, Aviation and Bomb Squad units; helicopters and drones; hundreds of NYPD and Sanitation blocker vehicles; **14,000 metal barricades**; a fully staffed joint operations center; camera and social-media monitoring; and "no known specific credible threats" [21]. Expect a similar or larger footprint for the 100th (ASSESSMENT). A private risk firm reports that the NYPD has said there is no known credible or specific threat to the 2026 event [22]; we have **not** found the underlying NYPD statement (SINGLE-SOURCE).
- **Parade history relevant to risk:**
    - **2016:** The ISIS magazine *Rumiyah* pictured the parade as "an excellent target" for a truck attack. The NYPD added blocker trucks and a counterterrorism overlay; no attack occurred [25].
    - **2023:** About 30 pro-Palestinian protesters jumped barricades and glued themselves to Sixth Ave near W 45th St. The parade was rerouted and 34 people were detained [24].
    - **2024:** 25 protesters were arrested after sitting down on the route [23].
    - **2025:** We found no reports of protest disruption.
    - **Wind:** In 1997, 43 mph winds drove the Cat in the Hat balloon into a lamppost and several people were injured. Weather grounded all balloons only once, in 1971 [26].
- **[ASSESSMENT]** The hardened route (blockers, barricades, screened viewing streets) makes a vehicle attack inside the route very difficult. The softer areas are the unscreened side streets, subway exits, and dispersal around noon, when millions leave at once, likely into rain this year. Pickpocketing and theft are the most common crimes near Herald Square [22].

## 5. Travel Week, Black Friday, and Times Square / Midtown [FACT unless noted]

- **National:** AAA projects **81.5 million** Americans traveling 50+ miles from 24 to 30 Nov, about the same as 2025's record. INRIX says New York could see more than double normal congestion. Worst driving windows: **Wed 25 Nov 12–7 PM**; Sat/Sun 12–8/9 PM. Best: before noon [29].
- **Port Authority (23–30 Nov):** about **8.6 million** travelers across its airports, bridges and tunnels. That includes about **3.1 million** air passengers at JFK, Newark, LaGuardia and Stewart (3% below last year's record) and about 5.5 million vehicle crossings. Busiest days: **Wed 25 Nov and Mon 30 Nov**. Arrive 2 hours early for domestic flights and 3 for international. Bridge and tunnel construction pauses 5 AM Wed through 5 AM Mon. PATH runs a Saturday schedule on Thursday. Non-REAL-ID licenses will not clear TSA without a passport, Global Entry card, or the $45 ConfirmID process [30].
- **Already seeing disruption:** On 22 Nov, Newark departures averaged about 105-minute delays and LaGuardia was under a ground stop, then a ground delay program, because of weather. The FAA's 13 Nov nationwide ground stop was caused by a contractor's network equipment problem [1][31].
- **Penn Station / Grand Central:** LIRR runs a weekend schedule on Thursday with extra parade trains; no alcohol on LIRR from 8 PM Wed to noon Thu. Metro-North runs a Friday schedule Wednesday with extra early-afternoon trains, and a special schedule Thursday. Select Penn Station entrances are closed on parade morning [20]. We found **no public reporting of a specific threat** to Penn Station, Grand Central, airports, bridges or tunnels this week.
- **Black Friday (27 Nov):** [ASSESSMENT] Herald Square/34th St, which is also the parade finish, is usually one of the busiest shopping areas, with the heaviest foot traffic from late morning to early evening. Most MTA buses run a reduced weekday schedule; LIRR off-peak fares apply all day [20].
- **Next big Midtown event:** The Rockefeller Center tree lighting is **Wed 2 Dec, 7–10 PM ET**. Expect security checkpoints, street closures, and crowds building from early afternoon [32].
- **Times Square / Midtown street crime:** On 8 Nov a man was slashed in a dispute at Broadway and W 46th St (minor injuries) [36]. This followed a cluster of knife attacks in Times Square in late August and early September, including a fatal random stabbing [35]. [ASSESSMENT] The typical Midtown risk is random or dispute-driven street violence and theft, concentrated late at night and in crowds.

## 6. Parade-Day Weather [FACT, forecasts as of 23 Nov; they will change]

- A **nor'easter** moves up the coast Wednesday into Thursday. The parade should start dry, with winds rising through the morning [26][28].
- **Wind:** AccuWeather forecasts east-northeast winds of 12–25 mph with 20–30 mph gusts during the parade. NBC 4 says gusts could hit 35 mph or more by **10–11 AM**, forcing balloons down, with peak gusts of 40–50 mph Thursday afternoon and evening. Balloons must fly low or be grounded above **23 mph sustained or 34 mph gusts** [26][27].
- **Rain:** Starting around late morning to midday, then heavy into Thursday night. Totals of **1–3 inches**, possible street and subway-entrance flooding, scattered power outages, and moderate to major **coastal flooding** at high tide (king tides) [26][28]. Choppy Hudson and East River ferry rides are possible [26].
- **[ASSESSMENT]** The wind (falling debris, balloon handling) and a wet, rushed dispersal are the most likely sources of injuries on parade day. Thursday afternoon and evening travel and flights out of NYC are likely to be disrupted.

## 7. NYE Outlook (brief) [ASSESSMENT]

The escalation window has moved from "after the midterms" to "**after the new year**," if Trump's signals hold [5][6]. That puts NYE (Times Square) right next to a possible restart of strikes. Watch for: a resumed U.S./Israeli campaign, an NTAS bulletin, any confirmation of the reported Iranian plotting [1][2], Hanukkah (4–12 Dec), and how the NYPD's post-SRG structure performs at the tree lighting and on NYE. Re-assess on 1 Dec and 28 Dec.

## 8. Recommendations for the iHeartRadio Team (non-operational)

- **Getting to work Thursday:** Sixth Ave is closed in Midtown from early morning to 3 PM, and W 42nd and W 57th Sts are closed from Fifth to Eighth Aves. Crossing Sixth Ave on foot will be limited while the parade passes. Arrive before about 6 AM or after about 1 PM, or use stations away from the route (the MTA suggests 47–50 Sts–Rockefeller Center as an alternative to the closed 57 St station) [17][20].
- **Rally points:** Choose a primary and a backup meeting spot **away** from Sixth Ave, Columbus Circle, Herald Square and the subway exits on the route, ideally indoors given the rain (for example, a team member's office lobby). Agree on a time to regroup after noon, when the dispersal starts.
- **Communications:** Agree on check-in times and a backup channel (text plus a messaging app) in case cell networks congest. Sign up for Notify NYC alerts. Follow @NYPDnews, NYC Emergency Management, and the MTA app for closures.
- **Travel timing:** Leave the city before noon on Wed 25 Nov or travel Thursday morning before the storm. Avoid Thursday afternoon and evening if possible. Check flight status before leaving; allow 2–3 hours at the airport. Carry a REAL ID or passport [29][30].
- **What to avoid:** umbrellas, backpacks and large bags on parade streets (wear waterproof gear instead); standing under balloon paths or near scaffolding and loose signage when gusts rise; the packed blocks next to the route at dispersal; low-lying waterfront streets during high tide Thursday; and flying any drone (prohibited) [18][19][26].
- **Information hygiene:** Treat "sleeper cell," "dirty bomb in NYC," or "cyberattack" posts as unverified unless the NYPD, DHS or FBI confirms them. Do not broadcast them without confirmation [1][2].
- **Cyber:** Expect holiday and Black Friday phishing and fake delivery texts. Use MFA on station and social accounts.
- **Report** suspicious activity to an officer, 911, or 1-888-NYC-SAFE.
